📘 Portfolio Reflection – 8‑3 Journal Submission
1. How I Approach Designing Software
Working through this project strengthened my ability to design software in a structured and intentional way. I learned how to break a complex visual goal into smaller components such as object classes, materials, lighting systems, and camera controls. My design process followed an iterative pattern: start with simple primitives, refine shapes and transformations, introduce lighting, and then adjust materials and shading to achieve realism.
In my current role as an apprentice optician, I regularly explain lens designs, coatings, and optical concepts to patients. This project helped me recognize how 3D visualization and clear structural thinking can support patient education—whether it’s illustrating how progressive lenses work or showing how contact lenses sit on the eye. The same design tactics I used here—modularity, clarity, and step‑by‑step refinement—translate directly into how I communicate optical concepts and solve problems in a retail healthcare setting.

2. How I Approach Developing Programs
Developing the 3D scene pushed me to adopt new development strategies centered around incremental building and testing. Iteration played a major role: I added one feature, verified it worked, and then layered the next. This was especially important for lighting, materials, and camera movement, where small adjustments created large visual differences.
Over the milestones, my coding approach evolved from simply “getting things to work” to writing cleaner, well‑commented, modular code that could be reused and extended. This mirrors the way I approach patient care and optical troubleshooting—small adjustments, careful testing, and refining until the result is correct. Whether I’m adjusting a frame fit, verifying a prescription, or solving a comfort issue with contact lenses, the iterative mindset I developed in this project directly supports my ability to diagnose and resolve problems in my day‑to‑day work.

3. How Computer Science Helps Me Reach My Goals
Computational graphics and visualization gave me hands‑on experience with concepts that support both my educational and professional goals. Academically, this project deepened my understanding of 3D math, rendering pipelines, lighting models, and object‑oriented design—skills that will carry forward into future CS courses.
Professionally, this project helped me see how computer science can enhance the optical field. Many modern optical tools—lens simulators, corneal topography systems, contact lens fitting software, and patient education apps—use 3D graphics to visualize optical surfaces and eye anatomy. The skills I gained here give me a foundation for understanding and potentially contributing to those technologies in the future. This project showed me how computer science can turn abstract ideas into interactive, visual results, and that’s a skill I can apply both in my continued education and in the healthcare environment where I work.
